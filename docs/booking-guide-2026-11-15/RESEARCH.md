# True Path Research Brief

Topic: Home Service Booking Rate.
Assumptions: Owner-operated service business. The 30-inquiry example is fictional; no customer data or measured client result is supplied. Trevor authorized completion and publication of the next priority article using the previous page's workflow.

## 1. Best Practical Thesis

Follow the last thirty inquiries to their current outcomes before choosing whether the business needs more demand, better handling, or more capacity.

## 2. Why This Matters Now

The current published article reverses the formula in a FAQ, applies unsourced booking thresholds, and treats unbooked requests as lost work. Current platform documentation also shows that calls and reported conversions can use definitions that do not establish booked appointments.

## 3. Best Proof Points

- [ServiceTitan Modular Dashboard definitions](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/dashboard-business-metrics), checked November 15, 2026: call-lead classification and report scope affect the denominator. The draft explicitly labels its broader cross-channel inquiry audit as a different measure.
- [Google Business Profile metrics](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en): call-button activity should not be reported as confirmed appointments.
- [Google Ads call reporting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7180997?hl=en): duration-based call conversions are distinct from observed booking outcomes.
- Local arithmetic: 40 contact records minus 6 repeat contacts and 4 spam/unrelated records leaves 30 eligible inquiries; 12 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 30; 12/30 = 40% so far; 6 + 3 = 9 still open. These are invented teaching numbers, not research estimates.

## 4. Service-Business Examples

- Plumbing: repeat calls and a form for one water heater remain one opportunity.
- HVAC: suitable work turned away because no appointment is available remains visible as a capacity constraint.
- Remodeling: a planned-project inquiry needs a different decision window from an urgent repair.

## 5. Best Content Angle

Explain the unbooked portion of the inquiry list. A visual outcome map plus a blank worksheet makes the article useful at the office desk instead of offering another generic lead-response lecture.

## 6. Hooks

- Where did the last thirty inquiries go?
- A booking percentage leaves the next action unexplained.
- A customer still deciding is not a confirmed lost job.

## 7. Audit Tie-In

Record one row per distinct request, eligibility, response time, current status, known reason, owner, next action, and review date. Keep eligibility unknowns separate. Compare groups with consistent definitions and decision windows.

## 8. Weak Advice To Avoid

- “Below 50% means bad handling” becomes a review of actual causes and job mix.
- “Respond in five minutes and book 100 times more” is removed; operating targets are not research-backed booking promises.
- “Add lead spend when already at capacity” becomes a capacity and job-mix decision.
- “Unbooked means lost” becomes explicit open, capacity, and declined outcomes.
- “One unanswered follow-up means no” becomes a customer-agreed sequence and a known status.
