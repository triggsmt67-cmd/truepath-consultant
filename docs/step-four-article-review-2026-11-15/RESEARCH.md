## True Path Research Brief — Booking

Topic: Home Service Booking Rate.
Assumptions: Owner-operated service business; no verified benchmark or client result supplied.

### 1. Best Practical Thesis
Count unique suitable opportunities and their outcomes before deciding whether the schedule needs more inquiries, better handling, or more capacity.

### 2. Why This Matters Now
The current article reverses the formula in its FAQ and encourages increased demand when crews are already full. These are operational errors regardless of platform changes.

### 3. Best Proof Points
- The captured article contradicts its own formula — `before/home-service-booking.html` — correct both locations.
- The denominator changes when spam and repeat contacts are counted — explicit arithmetic in the hypothetical example — make eligibility visible.
- [Google's profile metric definitions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) do not establish booked jobs — use an opportunity record for that outcome.

### 4. Service-Business Examples
- Plumbing: repeat calls about one leak remain one opportunity.
- HVAC: a suitable request declined for lack of capacity stays visible.
- Remodeling: an open decision needs longer observation than an urgent repair.

### 5. Best Content Angle
A cohort ledger beats a universal percentage target because it shows which constraint needs work.

### 6. Hooks
- A thin schedule has more than one possible cause.
- Forty phone contacts may not be forty opportunities.
- Know what happened to the requests you already received.

### 7. Audit Tie-In
Review 30 eligible inquiries, record status and known reasons, and choose one constraint to address.

### 8. Weak Advice To Avoid
- “Under 50% is bad” → compare consistent job types and decision windows.
- “More leads at full capacity” → establish room and economics for additional work.
- “One unanswered contact means lost” → keep pending and unknown outcomes visible.

### 9. Recommended Next Skill
blog-post-writer: turn the measured diagnostic into a practical replacement draft.

### 5. Best Content Angle

### 6. Hooks

### 8. Weak Advice To Avoid

- Mandatory photo counts → relevant real work images.
- Service-area expansion claims → accurate operating footprint.

### 9. Recommended Next Skill

## True Path Research Brief — Estimate Follow-Up

Topic: Estimate Follow-Up.
Assumptions: Preserve the current title and URL; sequences are suggested experiments.

### 1. Best Practical Thesis
Agree on the next decision and contact instead of interpreting silence or repeating empty check-ins.

### 2. Why This Matters Now
This article earned the site's only captured 28-day click. Correct its claims while preserving its useful purpose and URL.

### 3. Best Proof Points
- Saved performance shows 2 clicks/154 impressions at the canonical URL over three months.
- Captured copy contains source placeholders and an unconfirmed contractor story.
- The current Booking article's one-touch rule conflicts with this article — use one consistent workflow.

### 4. Service-Business Examples
- HVAC: clarify installation inclusions before comparing price.
- Roofing: follow the homeowner's agreed decision date.
- Landscaping: separate a spring project from urgent repair timing.

### 5. Best Content Angle
Provide actual messages and stop rules, not a new response-time statistic.

### 6. Hooks
- An estimate needs a next step as well as a number.
- Silence isn't a reliable loss reason.
- Make the next contact worth answering.

### 7. Audit Tie-In
Assign an owner, status, and next action to ten open estimates; track issued estimates as a group.

### 8. Weak Advice To Avoid
- “Price is rarely the issue” → ask rather than assume.
- A universal fast-follow-up lift → measure your own quote cohort.
- Endless automation → stop on response, decline, booking, or opt-out.

### 9. Recommended Next Skill
blog-post-writer: preserve the uncertainty argument and add the missing operational sequence.

## True Path Research Brief — Reviews Process

Topic: Reviews Process.
Assumptions: User-confirmed phrase; original firsthand account is unverified.

### 1. Best Practical Thesis
Build a reliable invitation process that makes honest feedback easy without steering the customer.

### 2. Why This Matters Now
The current workflow has useful mechanics but includes sentiment screening and prescribed-content advice.

### 3. Best Proof Points
- [Review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) defines the solicitation limits.
- [Google's review-request help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) supports a direct feedback route.
- [Twilio policy](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/legal/messaging-policy) provides a concrete provider example for automation requirements.

### 4. Service-Business Examples
- Auto repair: an optional counter sign and an assigned process owner.
- HVAC: a request after the customer has had time to experience the work.
- Cleaning: the same objective invitation rule regardless of expected sentiment.

### 5. Best Content Angle
Measure invitation consistency and delivery rather than demanding staff generate reviews or chasing named customers.

### 6. Hooks
- The review request needs an owner.
- A QR code in a text can make the easy step harder.
- Check the link before coaching the front desk again.

### 7. Audit Tie-In
Check twenty completed jobs for eligibility consistency, permissions, delivery, and working links.

### 8. Weak Advice To Avoid
- Review keyword prompts → let customers choose their words.
- Happy-only invitations → objective selection rules.
- Review quotas and pressure → optional participation and a reliable process.

### 9. Recommended Next Skill
seo-optimizer: retain the useful process and remove unsupported ranking claims from every publishing field.
