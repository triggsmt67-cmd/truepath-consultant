Marketing can stall at several different points: the ad doesn't show, the wrong person clicks, the page doesn't answer their question, or nobody handles the inquiry. Find the broken handoff before changing the whole campaign.

Clear copy matters. So do delivery settings, budget, competition, a working page, and reliable follow-through. Blaming every weak result on the message can send you looking in the wrong place.

First, check whether the campaign can deliver

If an ad barely shows, check approval status, billing, dates, location targeting, budget, and bidding constraints before rewriting the headline. A good offer can't fix an ineligible ad.

Google's Ad Rank documentation explains that placement depends on several factors, including bids, auction context, competition, and ad and landing-page quality. Better creative is one part of that system. It doesn't guarantee more distribution.

Avoid applying one platform's auction explanation to every other channel. Use the delivery diagnostics available in the account you're reviewing.

Then follow the promise from the ad to the page

Take one real ad and open its destination on your phone. Write down the service, location, and next step the ad promises. Then look for those same details on the page.

Here's a hypothetical HVAC example:

Ad: “Furnace repair in Missoula. Call to check today's availability.”

Weak handoff: a general homepage about heating, cooling, installations, and company history, with the phone number buried below several sections.

Clearer handoff: a furnace-repair page that confirms the service area, explains the diagnostic visit, shows the phone number, and tells the customer how availability is confirmed.

The clearer version answers the request the ad attracted. It still can't promise a booking, and it shouldn't advertise immediate service when the schedule is full.

Keep the message-match checklist

Before changing bids or ordering more creative, check:

  • Can someone explain the offer after reading one sentence?
  • Does the page name the service and area shown in the ad?
  • Does it answer the obvious question about scope, availability, or the next step?
  • Is there relevant proof you can substantiate?
  • Can a phone user call or submit the form without hunting?
  • Does someone receive and handle the inquiry?

Test the form with an identifiable test inquiry and confirm it arrives. A successful screen message doesn't prove the office received it. Don't include test entries in your lead totals.

Read the counts before naming the cause

Use the same date range and keep these measures separate:

  • Impressions: the ad was shown.
  • Clicks: someone selected it.
  • Recorded inquiries: calls or forms that reached the business.
  • Suitable inquiries: requests for work you can actually provide.
  • Booked work: an opportunity became a confirmed booking.

If clicks arrive but recorded inquiries don't, inspect the page and contact route. If inquiries arrive but most are unsuitable, inspect targeting and the offer. If suitable inquiries arrive but sit unanswered, inspect the response process.

These patterns suggest where to investigate. Missing tracking can produce the same apparent gap as poor performance. Don't report zero inquiries when nobody has measured them.

Give the experiment one job

Choose the clearest break and write down what you'll change. For example: “Replace the general homepage destination with the relevant repair page, using the same service promise.” Record the dates and keep other major changes limited where practical.

Review enough activity to understand the result. A few clicks can't tell you which headline wins, and a busy or quiet week can reflect weather, seasonality, or staffing as well as marketing.

If the page is the weak point, start with making the website's next step obvious. If suitable inquiries aren't becoming appointments, check your home service booking rate.

Pick one active ad today. Follow it through to a received inquiry and an assigned person. That is a more useful starting point than changing everything in Ads Manager at once.
