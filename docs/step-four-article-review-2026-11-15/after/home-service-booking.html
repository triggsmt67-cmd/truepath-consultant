Before buying more leads, check how many suitable inquiries become booked jobs. Count each opportunity once, define what qualifies, and track the result. A thin schedule can come from too few inquiries, poor fit, slow response, or a lack of available appointments. Those need different fixes.

Your booking rate helps you choose the next move. It doesn't diagnose the whole business by itself.

Calculate your home service booking rate with a clear denominator

For this audit, use booked opportunities ÷ eligible inquiries × 100. An eligible inquiry is a genuine request for a service you offer, in an area you serve. Include suitable work you couldn't schedule; capacity is part of what you're checking.

Count one customer's request once, even if they call twice and submit a form. Exclude spam, vendor calls, and duplicate contacts. Record the exclusions so next month's number uses the same rules.

Here's a hypothetical example: 40 inbound contacts include six repeat contacts and four spam or unrelated requests. That leaves 30 eligible inquiries. If 12 become bookings, the rate is 12 ÷ 30 × 100 = 40%.

That does not mean 60% of the ad budget was wasted. Inquiries have different sources, costs, job values, and outcomes. It means 18 suitable opportunities didn't become bookings within the period you measured.

Follow one group of inquiries through to an outcome

Start with inquiries received during one week. Give each one a row in a notebook or spreadsheet:

  • Date received and source, if known.
  • Service requested and whether it qualifies.
  • Time of first human response.
  • Booked, awaiting a decision, declined, unreachable, or no capacity.
  • Reason for the outcome, when the customer actually gives one.

Don't count an unanswered call as a lost job or assume a silent customer rejected your price. Keep unknowns visible. Review the same group again after the normal decision window for that type of work.

A furnace repair request and a planned bathroom remodel shouldn't share an arbitrary seven-day deadline. Split urgent service and planned projects before comparing rates.

Find the constraint before choosing the fix

Suitable inquiries are going unanswered: assign call coverage or a callback owner. Set a response target your team can safely meet, and measure actual response times. A target is an operating choice, not a universal promise about booking lift.

Customers are asking for work you don't do: check the ad, profile, and page that generated the inquiry. Clearer service boundaries may help more than faster callbacks.

Customers want dates you can't offer: review capacity and scheduling. Buying more of the same inquiries may add cost without creating room for the work.

Estimates are waiting on a decision: use a separate estimate follow-up process. Agree on the next contact and make it useful; one unanswered message doesn't explain why a job is still open.

Make the callback useful

When you return a call, identify yourself, confirm the service needed, and explain the next available step. Don't promise same-day service unless you can deliver it.

For a customer who has agreed to text about the request, a starting message could be:

Hi [first name], this is [name] at [company], returning your service inquiry. What do you need help with, and when is a good time to talk?

Use the customer's agreed contact channel. Don't build automatic texts around a phone number alone.

Decide whether more lead spend makes sense

There is no single booking percentage that tells every trade to increase its budget. Look at available capacity, suitable inquiry volume, booked-job cost, and whether the work is profitable.

Change one part of the process, then review another comparable group of inquiries. Record the dates and counts alongside the percentage. A small week's result is a clue to investigate, not proof of a permanent improvement.

Start with your last 30 eligible inquiries. If you can't tell what happened to them, fixing that record is useful work before the next budget decision. The seven-question marketing checkup can help you choose where to look first.
